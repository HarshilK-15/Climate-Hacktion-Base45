Shipless: funding summary for 7 Pacific island microgrids

This portfolio puts solar panels and batteries on 7 island grids for AUD 2,918,560. In a typical year it saves 270,398 litres of diesel and 725 tonnes of CO2, and 7 of 7 clinics keep their vaccine fridges powered through a 7-day fuel-ship delay in the darkest week on record.

What the money returns each year

  • 270,398 litres of diesel not burned in a typical year (P50); at least 265,007 litres in a bad year (the sum of each island's P90, beaten 9 years out of 10, which is conservative for a portfolio).
  • AUD 521,973 a year of diesel not bought (typical year).
  • 725 tonnes of CO2 avoided a year.
  • 7 of 7 clinics keep their vaccine fridge and emergency light powered for 7 days with no diesel at all, in the darkest week of 20 years of NASA weather.

Why you can trust these numbers

  • Every island is simulated hour by hour on 20 years of real NASA satellite weather, and every design must never black out in any of those years.
  • Checked against a real island: for Ta'u (American Samoa) our fuel model turns its reported diesel into 1,263,778 kWh a year of electricity against 1,300,000 kWh reported (2.8% apart), and our engine runs its as-built 1,410 kW solar and 6,000 kWh battery at 99.7% solar.
  • Prices tested 30% up and down (diesel and solar): solar still beats diesel on every island in every case, by at least AUD 129,505 over 20 years. On 2 of 7 islands the design barely changes; where diesel is cheapest, a big fall in the diesel price would favour a smaller battery, so lock in the fuel price before ordering.

What these numbers do not include

Load growth, panel ageing, diesel price changes over time, cyclone damage and generator replacement. Household counts and loads are illustrative until each island's own data is in.
